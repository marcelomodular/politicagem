Título extraído: Who gets to decide Venezuela’s future?

In the days after the military operation that ousted Nicolás Maduro, a poll from the Caracas‑based firm Meganálisis showed that 92 percent of Venezuelans were grateful to President Donald Trump for removing Maduro and that 90 percent wanted the US as Venezuela’s main ally. During the months since, the US has been heavily involved in shaping Venezuela’s future. But some key reforms are moving slower than Venezuelans hoped for.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio has laid out a three-step plan for US intervention in Venezuela: stability, recovery, and a transition to democracy. While plans for political reforms and democratic elections in Venezuela are moving slowly, the designs for who controls its oil have been moving fast. The US recently announced an agreement with Venezuela as “the biggest oil deal in world history.”

As Venezuelans hold out hope that the final step in Rubio’s plan, a transition to democracy, will still materialize, support among Venezuelans for Trump appears to be slipping. According to a new Meganálisis poll, 83 percent of Venezuelans don’t believe Trump wants what is best for their country. As voices grow louder from opposition leaders like Nobel Peace Prize winner María Corina Machado to jumpstart democratic processes, pressure on the US is mounting to make good on their promise.
