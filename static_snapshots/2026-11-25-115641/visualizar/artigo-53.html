Título extraído: 2026 Gift Guide for Global Nerds, Travelers, Foodies

It’s been a rough year for travel. Oil prices skyrocketed after Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz at the start of the U.S.-Israeli war on the country, leaving many people thinking twice about the steep cost of airfare. More recent disruptions, including to U.S. air traffic control, made us determined to curate a list of holiday gift recommendations that will make you feel globally connected from the comfort of your own home.

So Brick your phone, set tea to steep with a gooseneck kettle, put on a Mongolian cashmere hoodie, and cozy up to complete a British paint-by-numbers kit while munching on Spanish jamón chips. (Fear not: Those still inclined to travel will also find our favorite bags and gadgets.)

Baggu Medium Nylon Crescent Bag ($56)

I’ll be the first to admit that I have a big problem—er, a bag problem. My closet is practically overflowing with thrifted purses, and my FP colleagues know that I come into the office carrying not one or two sensible totes, but three or four. So trust me when I say that I know bags, and this one stands above the rest.

Baggu’s Medium Nylon Crescent Bag is a workhorse, ideal for a friend or loved one looking for a trusty companion for weekend errands, jet-setting adventures, and everything in between. I’ve previously recommended Baggu’s packing cubes for their durability and cute patterns. This product has those same qualities.

Sleek and roomy, the crescent bag is available in a range of colors, materials (mine is leather), and sizes, although I’ve found that the medium-sized pouch is perfect for my needs.

—Christina Lu, staff writer

JisuLife Portable Handheld Fan Ultra2 ($72.67, currently on sale)

I’ve traveled to a bunch of countries over the years, but my luggage tag collection is not the largest at FP—not by a long shot. Many of my colleagues have been to more destinations and stayed longer, just one of the things that makes working here so delightful. In the office, everyone has a travel tip.

Mine: The gadgets you take with you should be multifunctional. The exemplar of an all-purpose gadget is this freakishly powerful fan that’s also a power bank, flashlight, and handy-dandy keyboard cleaner. I keep it in my carry-on, fan myself before takeoff, charge my phone over the Atlantic, and light up the seat pocket on arrival to make sure I didn’t leave my passport behind. As for my keyboard—it’s reliably crumb-free.

—Dan Ephron, executive editor

Etnodim Ivory Wool Scarf “Zavii” ($122)

Etnodim makes high-quality, gorgeous interpretations of classical Ukrainian clothes, from embroidered shirts and dresses to traditional kerchiefs. On a trip to Kyiv this fall, I got a traditional shirt, known as a vyshyvanka, for my daughter from their brand store in Podil, Kyiv’s equivalent of Brooklyn. Fortunately, you don’t have to travel 30-plus hours by plane and train to get their items, which are also sold online.

While I’d heartily recommend Etnodim’s wildly colorful designs, their more basic items—like this merino wool scarf—go with anything. Best of all, you’ll be supporting a Ukrainian brand that celebrates the country’s unique identity, in opposition to Russia’s best efforts to stamp out such cultural expressions.

—Sam Skove, staff writer

Breville Milk Café milk frother ($199.95)

After realizing just how much money I was spending per month on lattes at my local (international chain) coffee shop, I decided to look into making them at home. But I didn’t want to spend thousands of dollars on some behemoth machine that requires a specialist degree to operate, either.

Enter the Breville Milk Café milk frother. It’s super easy to use and clean, it doesn’t take up much counter space, and it reliably makes the perfect steamed milk to my desired temperature and amount of foam. I’ve used it at least once—if not twice—a day for the last five months, and it hasn’t let me down yet.

But that’s just the milk part of the equation. Now on to the coffee part…

Breville Nespresso Original Essenza Mini Espresso Machine ($174.99)

The Nespresso Original Essenza Mini Espresso Machine is basically a Keurig for espresso. You don’t have to grind beans or fiddle with a million buttons and levers to make a single shot of espresso—just pop in a Nespresso pod; make sure the little, easily removable water tank in the back is full; and hit a button.

This machine is also compact, so it doesn’t monopolize your kitchen. Best of all, the espresso tastes just as rich and creamy as it would if you went to the corner coffee shop—and you don’t even have to put on pants to enjoy it.

—Jennifer Williams, deputy editor

Ortlieb Vario Lite pannier backpack ($195)

FP recently moved offices to a space farther away from the D.C. Metro’s Red Line—and so I became a bike commuter.

Biking has long been my preferred mode of transportation on the weekends, when I shuttle between yoga and Trader Joe’s with the help of a storage basket. But I quickly realized that commuting is an entirely different ball game. Laptop plus lunch is a heavy—and valuable—combo. I did not trust my basket, however adorable, to keep these items secure.

Enter: a pannier backpack. Rather than settling either for a backpack (which would strain my back while biking and probably make me sweaty) or a pannier (which would be difficult to transport after parking my bike), I decided to invest in this brilliant product from Ortlieb, a German brand that happens to be—like me—Franconian. It has transformed my commute.

The Vario Lite allows me to toggle seamlessly between pannier and backpack modes. It has an interior laptop pocket and outer compartment where I store my keys and phone for easy access. Its rolltop structure means that its size is flexible, and its backpack function is ergonomic. Did I mention that it’s waterproof, too?

The Vario Lite is the little brother of the Vario, a slightly larger pannier backpack from Ortlieb. I tried on both and found the Vario Lite to be a better fit on my 5’1” frame. But anyone can rest assured that there’s an entire line of high-quality pannier backpacks—in a range of colors—to choose from.

—Allison Meakem, senior editor

Brick Phone Distraction Blocker ($56.80, currently on sale)

The Brick—a magnetic square you can use to curtail access to your phone—is an invention I resisted for a long time. Its very existence was proof that we were living at capitalism’s nadir: You’re telling me I need to buy a little plastic brick to blunt the pernicious effects of this other little plastic brick?

And yet. The Brick has made my life better, and here are the numbers to back up that claim. Before acquiring the device, I was averaging 10 hours and 40 minutes on my phone per day. Now that number is down to just under eight hours. Sometimes, when there isn’t an international spy scandal all over the news, it has been known to hit seven.

That I have been wasting this much of my life scrolling is mortifying enough. What’s worse is how easy it was to break the habit. The minor inconvenience the Brick depends on is that once you block certain apps you can only unblock them by tapping your phone to the cube. Tapping the cube is very easy. But most of the time, it’s simply too much of a bother. So instead of doomscrolling, you put down the phone and do something else instead, such as engaging with your children or animals.

Turns out you can throw money at seemingly intractable problems. Capitalism, you win this round.

—Amelia Lester, deputy editor

Scotch Precision Ultra Edge Scissors ($11.39)

I, too, initially recommended the Brick (above) but voluntarily scrapped my write-up lest attention spans wane while reading too many words about a plastic square. Instead, I’d like to recommend something analogue to fill your newfound free time: paper collage.

I recently turned to physical collaging as a cathartic supplement to the digital work I sometimes do for FP. It’s the perfect hobby for the world traveler with piles of ephemera or magazine readers with a backlog of vintage Foreign Policy and National Geographic. Pull together a gift collage kit with paper bits of your choice and these sharp, precise scissors.

These superior snips come recommended secondhand by Ray Orr, a collage artist and former visual journalist and co-founder of Living Room DC, a creative space in D.C.’s Mount Pleasant neighborhood. Consider gifting a workshop or drop-in session for collage or artmaking in the cozy spot.

—Lori Kelley, executive creative director

World leaders may have decided to shirk responsibility for confronting our warming planet, but you can still control the temperature of water at home with this luxurious electric kettle. Everyman’s Library Pocket Classics ($16-$28)

If you’re like me, packing for a trip is a calculation of how much reading material you can bring to fill time spent in transit without losing precious real estate in your bag. “They’ll have bookstores there,” a wise friend once reminded me. But a smart traveler comes prepared!

Short story collections from Penguin Random House’s Everyman’s Library Pocket Classics Series are a compact travel companion and a great gift for someone you know who’s getting excited for an upcoming trip (or still reminiscing about their last one).

From India to Russia to Ireland, these anthologies compile literary short stories and book excerpts related to the featured country, continent, or city. Their contents can introduce you to a new destination, deepen your appreciation for a place you already love, or even inspire a change of itinerary.

I’ve found the anthology format to be ideal for the random stretches of time to kill when traveling between destinations. The stories are just the right length to digest on a plane, bus, or train ride, and can even make the inevitable delay more enjoyable.

—Maya Krainc, editorial fellow

Zeedog Ruff Leash ($15.20, currently on sale)

This year, I became the owner of a young dog—and also the continued owner of pair of aging arms. After having my shoulder wrenched by my puppy’s eagerness to chase D.C.’s rats a few too many times, I switched to this leash from Zeedog.

Through a simple shock-absorbing design, which looks like a stretchy wave at the end of the leash, the Ruff Leash absorbs the force when your dog yanks. It also comes, like puppies, in a stylish variety of colors. Strongly recommended for anyone foolish enough, like me, to get one.

Liberty Puzzles ($30-$435)

A confession: I have long known what the perfect holiday gift is, but I’ve kept it to myself. No more! It’s a puzzle from Liberty Puzzles, a wooden jigsaw puzzle made in Boulder, Colorado. (Full disclosure: I worked at Liberty Puzzles long ago, but unlike my friends at fast-food joints, who came away swearing off the menu, I left charmed having seen how the product gets made.)

What sets a Liberty Puzzle apart from the typical cardboard variety? For starters, the shapes. Each one is unusual, and thick, laser-cut pieces fall into place with a satisfying click. They also have a faint smell of burnt maple. Best of all, if you ever lose a piece—say, in the stomach of a mischievous furry friend—the company will replace it for free.

The catalog is quite global, including landscapes, art, maps, and vintage posters, so there’s something for everyone. I’ll make it easy for you: A gastronomical map of Italy for the couple fresh back from their honeymoon! A woodblock print of Japan’s snow-dusted Mount Haruna for the burgeoning snowboarder! A painting of Diana and her nymphs for the Roman history buff in your life! They even make kids’ puzzles.

And if you, an FP subscriber, are shopping for yourself, I suspect the Voyage Round the World poster would fit the bill.

Torres Selecta Iberian Ham Gourmet Potato Chips ($49)

Give the candy canes a break this year and stuff your stockings with chips!

I first encountered these pork-flavored potato chips at my neighborhood wine bar years ago, and they quickly became a cult favorite. They’ve been on my mind again, having recently returned from Andalusia, Spain, where jamón ibérico seemed to show up at every meal.

Salty, savory, and downright decadent, these chips would pair great with a dollop of creme fraiche; a dab of fig jam, pickled shallots, or salmon roe; and a glass of cava. They’re the perfect treat for the adventurous eater on your list, or anyone who’s still dreaming of their last trip.

—Megan DuBois, associate editor

Bic 4-Color Original Retractable Ballpoint Pen ($4.38, currently on sale)

One of the dangers of having kids is the constant temptation to indulge in nostalgia for the objects of one’s own youth. But sometimes nostalgia can be an invitation to genuine rediscovery.

Such was my experience with the classic Bic four-color pen. I bought one for my fifth-grade son during this year’s back-to-school run-up and watched as his newfound power dawned. You’re writing in blue, and with a flick of the thumb: Boom! Green. If the mood strikes: Boom! Back to blue—or red?

I soon bought a four-color pen for myself and quickly came to a renewed appreciation for its simple design and small daily offer of variety. But reading about the pen’s history also gave me a deeper understanding of its geopolitical significance. I hadn’t known that Bic is a French company, and that the iconic four-color pen has always—since its original release in 1970—been a product proudly made in France. The factory in the town of Montévrain still closely guards its manufacturing secrets.

The Bic four-color pen isn’t just a writing implement—it’s a precisely engineered machine, an enduring symbol of Francofuturism. All of which makes it the perfect stocking stuffer for the status-conscious middle schooler in your life—or the wonky Europhilic aesthete.

—Cameron Abadi, deputy editor

The Oregon Trail Card Game ($18.50)

Although I have never been much of a video gamer, I would be lying if I said that the 1990s classic Oregon Trail did not eat up hours of my childhood. Fighting to survive bandits, broken axels, and (of course) dysentery was a favorite activity of mine when the days were cold, the nights were long, and I needed an escape from our chaotic world.

So when I was gifted a physical version of the iconic game, I was hooked. The story is the same: Make your way from Independence, Missouri, to Willamette Valley, Oregon, without succumbing to drowning, starvation, or a host of other calamities. My friends and I have spent hours around my coffee table attempting to cross the treacherous 2,000-plus-mile passage without incident. There have been screaming matches, thrown cards, and more typhoid outbreaks than we can count.

And, of course, we have died every time.

—Alexandra Sharp, World Brief writer
