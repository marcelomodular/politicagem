Welcome to The Logoff: President Donald Trump is running out of time to get his nominees confirmed.

What’s happening? On Wednesday, outgoing Republican Sen. Bill Cassidy blocked Trump’s nominee to lead the National Science Foundation, Jim O’Neill, from confirmation. And last night, the White House pulled another nominee, would-be FDA commissioner Heidi Overton, from consideration ahead of a Wednesday committee vote.

Both come at the worst possible time for Trump, who’s facing a dwindling legislative calendar with a Republican Senate majority on its way out.

Cassidy, a physician, chairs the Senate health committee, which is responsible for approving both nominations before they go to the full Senate. On Tuesday evening, he wrote in a social media post that “unfortunately, with the limited time left, confirming a permanent FDA Commissioner will fall to Senate Democrats.”

What’s the context? A new Congress will be sworn in on January 3, in about a month and a half, and Democrats will hold sizable majorities in both chambers.

In reality, though, the Senate has less time than that to deal with new nominations: Not only are the holidays approaching, but Congress has other big-ticket priorities on its plate, including heading off another government shutdown and potential deals on permitting reform and the debt ceiling.

Why does this matter? Trump loves an acting appointment, but those officials can often only serve for so long in a role without Senate approval, which will be an uphill battle in a Senate with a 56-seat Democratic majority. (For perspective: Even with Republicans in control, some of his most controversial nominees, like Pete Hegseth and Robert F. Kennedy Jr., barely made it through. And incoming Sen.-elect Annie Andrews (D-SC) ran on impeaching Kennedy.)

The new Senate is also near certain to spell the end of Trump’s ability to confirm judges to lower courts or the Supreme Court for the remainder of his presidency.

In other words, time is of the essence and failed nominations like O’Neill and Overton are a particularly big deal. If Trump wants to fill those jobs, he’ll need to find a nominee that a new Democratic majority in the Senate is willing to confirm — I won’t be holding my breath.
