Welcome to The Logoff: The Trump administration is closing a global AI chip loophole that the Jensen Huang scandal put in the spotlight.

What’s changing? The Commerce Department is trying to make it harder for China to access top-of-the-line AI microchips like those made by Nvidia.

Currently, China is able to dodge export rules by renting computing power from third-country data centers, which can legally buy American-made chips that China can’t.

But under the rule issued Monday, which is set to take effect in 90 days, data centers in a handful of countries will face new export restrictions on chips — including a requirement that they perform “robust due diligence on their customers” to make sure that they aren’t evading an export ban.

What’s the context? Huang, who resigned from Nvidia last week, was brought down in part by his relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), an alleged Chinese spy (kind of) and an executive at a Singapore-based tech company, Megaspeed, that was renting compute to Chinese firms — exactly the kind of thing that the new rule aims to crack down on.

What’s the big picture? The Nvidia scandal is front and center. But under the hood, there’s also some Trump administration infighting going on. As a statement announcing the new rule notes, it was written not by the Commerce Department, which is issuing it, but by the Treasury Department and the National Security Council.

That’s conspicuous because Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has done his best to throw Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick under the bus for the scandal, saying that Lutnick’s department failed to restrict Megaspeed’s access to advanced AI chips sooner.

Where’s President Donald Trump in all this? As of Monday, off to China and the Philippines for a multiday trip that will feature a trilateral summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

For now, though, Trump is standing by Lutnick — and even by Jensen Huang, saying Monday that he “would certainly not mind” seeing him return as Nvidia CEO (the Nvidia board has already shut down the possibility).
